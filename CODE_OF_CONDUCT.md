# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person you are talking to is trying to help. Disagreeing about a
technical decision is useful; disparaging the person making it is not.

## What is expected

- Take other people's problems seriously, including the ones that seem obvious to you
- Give feedback on the work rather than the person
- Accept that people have different levels of experience, different first languages, and
  different amounts of time to spend
- Assume good faith when something reads badly. Tone travels poorly in text and across
  cultures

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in public or in private
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome attention of any kind
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions or deliberate derailment
- Any conduct that would reasonably make someone feel unwelcome contributing here
- Soliciting paid work in bulk, meaning quoting a price on several issues at once, or
  continuing to do so after being told no on any repository in this organisation

That last one is newer than the rest and worth explaining, because asking is not the problem.

These projects are unfunded and every contribution is voluntary, which the
[contributing guide](CONTRIBUTING.md) states plainly. One person asking one time whether there
is a budget gets a friendly no and nothing more. What is not acceptable is posting the same
priced offer across many issues, which costs a reply on every one of them, or carrying on
after the answer has already been given.

Issues in these repositories are deliberately written with the file, the line, the mechanism
and usually the fix already in them, so that someone unfamiliar with the codebase can start
without asking permission. A comment that restates that analysis and attaches a price has
returned nothing that was not already written down. Volume is what turns that from a question
into spam.

## Scope

This applies in every space these projects occupy: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, and any public communication where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Report anything that concerns you to **arnelirobles@gmail.com**.

Reports are read by the maintainer and kept confidential. You will get a response, and you
will not be asked to justify why something bothered you before it is taken seriously.

If the report concerns the maintainer, say so, and it will still be read and acted on.

## Enforcement

Depending on what happened, the response may be a private conversation, a public correction,
temporary removal from project spaces, or a permanent ban. The maintainer decides, and will
explain the reasoning to the person concerned.

For bulk solicitation specifically, the response is normally one reply declining, and then a
block from the organisation if it continues. The reply is the warning. Nobody is blocked for a
first question.
